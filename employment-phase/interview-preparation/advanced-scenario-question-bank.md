# Advanced Scenario Question Bank

Use **R-A-C-T**: Recognize, Assess, Communicate, Track. This is an interview structure, not a replacement for formal procedures.

## 1. Failed control test
- **Recognize:** Passing a failed control would compromise assurance integrity.
- **Assess:** Recheck criteria, evidence, period, exception, and impact.
- **Communicate:** Explain that the conclusion must reflect evidence; discuss approved classifications.
- **Track:** Record test steps, exception, owner, remediation, deadline, and escalation.

## 2. Missing audit evidence
- **Recognize:** Implementation cannot be validated from verbal confirmation alone.
- **Assess:** Clarify required evidence, period, alternative sources, and responsible teams.
- **Communicate:** Request specific evidence and explain its purpose without accusation.
- **Track:** Record requests, deadlines, limitations, and final conclusion.

## 3. Third-party vendor risk
- **Recognize:** Sensitive-data processing creates confidentiality, integrity, availability, privacy, and continuity exposure.
- **Assess:** Review data, access, subcontractors, geography, contracts, assurance, incidents, continuity, and remediation.
- **Communicate:** Present facts, unknowns, options, compensating controls, and risk-acceptance authority.
- **Track:** Document findings, owner, actions, deadlines, reassessment, and escalation.

## 4. Immediate and follow-up vendor actions
Immediate actions may include restricting unnecessary access, requesting missing evidence, involving procurement/security/privacy/legal, and checking contractual obligations. Follow-up actions include remediation, assigned owners, deadlines, reassessment, and documented residual-risk decisions.

## 5. Policy noncompliance
- **Recognize:** Repeated failures may indicate awareness, usability, process, access, or enforcement problems.
- **Assess:** Review requirement, population, frequency, causes, training, system design, and impact.
- **Communicate:** Report the pattern factually without unsupported blame.
- **Track:** Record findings, owner, corrective action, training/process changes, deadlines, and retesting.

## 6. Risk acceptance request
- **Recognize:** Significant risk acceptance without justification creates governance concerns.
- **Assess:** Request risk description, assets, likelihood, impact, controls, alternatives, constraints, duration, and residual risk.
- **Communicate:** Explain the required authority and risk appetite.
- **Track:** Record rationale, approver, conditions, expiry/review date, and monitoring.

## 7. Incident involving personal data
- **Recognize:** The issue may involve security, privacy, legal, operational, and regulatory consequences.
- **Assess:** Follow incident procedures, preserve evidence, identify systems/data, and distinguish facts from unknowns.
- **Communicate:** Notify designated incident response, security, legal, privacy, and management stakeholders.
- **Track:** Record timeline, decisions, evidence, impact assessment, notification analysis, and lessons learned.

## 8. Documented intent vs operating evidence
A policy demonstrates documented intent. Operating evidence demonstrates that the control actually operated during the relevant period. A policy alone does not prove operating effectiveness.

## 9. Conflicting business priorities
- **Recognize:** Launching with incomplete controls may create exposure.
- **Assess:** Identify missing requirements, impact, compensating controls, dependencies, and duration.
- **Communicate:** Present options and consequences without making the business decision personally.
- **Track:** Record open items, owner, authority, conditions, deadlines, monitoring, and acceptance if authorized.

## 10. Portfolio credibility
My projects were educational simulations, not paid client engagements. They demonstrate practice in risk registers, control mappings, evidence indexes, policies, and remediation tracking, but I do not present them as professional audit or consulting work.
